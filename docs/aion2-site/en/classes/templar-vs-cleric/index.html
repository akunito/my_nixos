Templar vs Cleric

Data region: Korea and Taiwan (10 months live) · Version: state as of 2026-09-14; Global launches 2026-10-05 with Season 1 · Reviewed: 2026-09-16 · Confidence: medium. Templar was rewritten twice in August and September on KR and nobody knows which version Global ships.

The short version

Section titled “The short version”
  • Both classes get into any group. Templar is the game’s only true tank and Cleric the only class with a resurrection. That is where the similarity ends.
  • For competitive legion PvP, the numbers favour Templar. After the 30% PvP damage cut (July 2026): Templar is S-tier in 1v1 arena (70.8% win rate, tied top with Gladiator), A in the Abyss (KDA 11.5, the only melee above B) and A in co-op arena (41%). Cleric is B in 1v1 (66%), A in the Abyss (11.36) and S in co-op (47.5%).
  • For solo evenings, also Templar. Cleric is last of nine in PvE damage (71.7 against a 100 baseline); Templar sits at 88.4. A Templar pulls 7-8 mobs and eats the pack; Cleric solo content is described flatly as “hell”.
  • Cleric is the harder class to play well, not the easier one. Difficulty scoring (rotation / survival / group responsibility / mastery ceiling): Templar 3/2/8/7, Cleric 4/3/9/8. The Cleric’s burden is other people’s mistakes.
  • Interface clutter is mostly class-neutral. Cleric adds permanent party-frame watching; Templar adds macro engineering: a correct animation-cancel chain is worth about 0.5-0.7M damage per rotation (5.5M vs 4.9M) and needs low ping. Europe to an EU server is fine.
  • Templar was just rewritten, twice, weeks before Global. The 2026-08-26 KR patch gated Judgment (about two thirds of Templar damage) behind four shield skills and cut its PvP coefficient by about 21 effective points; further passes landed on 2026-09-01 and the week after. Whether Global ships pre- or post-rework Templar is unknown.
  • The 09-17/18 playtest (cap 37, 12 hours) cannot answer the PvP question. The Abyss unlocks at 45; arenas, battlegrounds, raids, Nightmare and Ascension Trial are locked. It can answer: does the macro loop feel good, is holding aggro fun, is party-frame babysitting tolerable.
  • Being useful costs less on Cleric; being good costs less on Templar. Cleric stays valuable with imperfect gear and execution; Templar can run one hybrid PvE/PvP set instead of two, which is real Kinah.
  • Whichever you pick, level the other as an active alt from day one. Gear and progression are character-bound and switching mains later is slow.

What is contested

Section titled “What is contested”

Is a tank even needed? The biggest risk on Templar

At the KR launch the roles of Templar and Cleric “weren’t that significant” in group content, and NCsoft answered by redesigning dungeons, not the kits. A Korean player puts it more bluntly: even after the fix, Cleric and Chanter were always brought for the buffs, “as for tanking, completely unnecessary most of the time”. The mechanical reason: the timed manual block or dodge (Shift) lets any class take zero damage from a boss mechanic, and Templar threat generation is “still terrible” after two dedicated patches.

Against that: by August-September Templar sits A-tier on KR PvE leaderboards (105 of 800) and shows up in most Sanctuary parties, and the move to 10-player raids favours real tanks. Global launches with the redesigned 5-player dungeons and 10-player raids, so tanks should be wanted. But “is the tank a formality?” is still open in Korea after ten months, and no English source engages with it.

Healer or debuff maintainer? The Korean framing: Aion 2 borrows Lost Ark’s raid philosophy, “healing is just a garnish”. The Cleric’s job is keeping party-wide damage debuffs up and dealing damage, as much as topping bars. The English “plain healer” framing is wrong by that standard. Since the 2026-03-25 overhaul, healing scales with the Cleric’s attack power, so the hybrid heal-and-hit build is correct, not optional.

Does Cleric have crowd control? One source says it is the only class with none; another counts 11. The stigma data settles it halfway: Bind (75% chance, 10 s, up to 4 targets, 1 min) and Assault Mark. Real CC, but short, chance-based, and no peel against a melee already on you.

The Templar rework (August-September 2026). On 2026-08-26 a minimum skill-cancel time hit all classes, aimed at shield melee. Judgment became gated behind four shield skills (Shield Strike, Guardian’s Blow, Shield Charge, Ruinous Shield) with a 3 s window, and its PvP coefficient dropped from ~34% to ~20%. On 2026-09-01 left-click damage went up, the right-click combo was tweaked and Judgment and Punishment were buffed: “a reshuffle, not a buff or a nerf”. If Global ships the pre-08-26 Templar, Judgment is passively available and PvP damage is the older, higher coefficient. If post, the macro needs rebuilding and PvP burst is lower. Check on day one: does Judgment trigger off shield skills?

Templar in PvP: dominant or mediocre? At the KR launch it was “the PvP cheat class”; an Aion 1 veteran listed it with Gladiator and Assassin as overtuned. By July 2026 the data half-vindicates everyone: top of 1v1 and the best melee in the Abyss. Tier letters on this class are noise (S, A, B and C in the same month depending on the list); win rates and leaderboards are not.

Cleric in PvP: unkillable or free kill? “Undeniably really hard to kill” solo and recommended for 1v1 because it outlasts; “a walking punching bag” per another. 66% in 1v1 is B, below Chanter and Sorcerer. And a KR-era complaint that matters for farming the Abyss: healing your group in PvP earned no Abyss Points. Unresolved for Global; verify at launch.

How each one plays

Section titled “How each one plays”

Templar. The clearest class identity in the game and it registers within the first hour: sword, shield, walk into the fight, do not leave. You do not feel immortal (your bar moves), but you can stand in front of a boss for 20 seconds without repositioning while the Sorcerer scrambles for distance and the Assassin hunts for the back arc. Solo, that is not “slower” but a different farming shape: you pull seven or eight mobs and eat them all, where a Ranger handles one to three.

It is not a piano class, but it is not thoughtless either: your strong follow-ups unlock when you create a condition (a stun, a knockdown, a block proc), so the loop is condition → payoff rather than a fixed sequence. The real ceiling is mechanical in another sense: the whole high-end Templar conversation is about macro chains and animation cancelling, because Judgment uptime is the class’s damage, and stringing Shield Smite (2 s) + Warding Strike (2 s) + a Doom Shield reset (3 s) is what keeps it alive.

In a group you decide where the fight happens, which way the boss faces and whether the melee get their cleave: a good Templar is worth more than two DPS and a bad one is a wipe. That is either the appeal or the reason to walk away. In PvP the kit is unusually complete for a tank: pulls, stuns, an anti-heal debuff, an Exposed that strips block and evasion, and enough durability to still be in the fight after the opening five seconds. That is why it wins duels against classes that out-damage it.

Cleric. Aion 2’s Cleric is a battle-priest, not a back-line water fountain. You start with a mace and shield and an almost entirely offensive skill list; healing arrives as you level. Leveling is comfortable (Healing Light means you are never potion-anxious and there is enough AoE to farm packs) but slow, and it is measurably the slowest of the nine classes at killing things. Solo endgame content (Nightmare, Awakening) is where that bites.

The actual gameplay is anticipation, not reaction: you pre-shield before scripted damage, you decide which of five tools the incoming hit deserves, and between those decisions you weave damage to hit your contribution check. Clerics who only heal are the ones who fail those checks. Your irreplaceable asset is the resurrection, and it is worth more than it sounds: resurrection stones are mostly disabled in raids, and people die.

In PvP you are annoying to kill rather than dangerous, and your duel damage is genuinely bad: the class outlasts, it does not win. In group PvP that inverts completely: you neutralise the enemy burst, keep the front line standing and change the rhythm of a fight with one cooldown; co-op arena matchmaking punishes the team without you. The cost: you are the priority target in every fight, with the game’s weakest reliable escape.

Reading the two together. Templar carries responsibility you control: your pulls, your positioning, your defensive timings. Cleric carries responsibility you do not control: other people’s mistakes and the blame for them. With a legion and competitive PvP as priorities, a casual evening schedule and little patience for clutter, the numbers point at Templar: top duelling, the best melee in mass PvP, forgiving solo, one gear set. Cleric is the right call only if holding the group up appeals more than dictating the fight.

Mistakes and traps

Section titled “Mistakes and traps”
  • Do not build a pure-heal or a pure-DPS Cleric. The first fails contribution checks; the second loses to real DPS by design.
  • Do not stack buffs that do not stack. Cleric’s Light of Protection is cancelled by a Chanter’s Undefeated Mantra; Templar’s Fury by a Gladiator’s Experience Counter Strike.
  • Do not macro a charge skill. Cleric’s Bolt fires one uncharged hit inside a macro; let it reach the third charge.
  • Do not push the Templar macro rate past your ping. At ~200 ms the cancel macro loses to pressing Judgment alone.
  • Do not chain immunities. Since 2026-03-25 two immunity effects cannot be used within 30 s of each other, on every class.
  • Do not judge either class by a tier letter. Win rates and leaderboard presence are far more stable.
  • Do not treat the playtest as a class-power test. At level 37 with the endgame locked you cannot see either class’s real job.
  • Do not plan on rerolling. There is no class change; switching means a new character from zero, with character-bound gear.
  • Do not buy a Founder’s Pack for power. Above Standard everything is cosmetic; the real content is 5 days of Early Access and 30 days of membership.
  • Do not go F2P and expect to trade. Without the ~$15 membership there is no market and no player trading.
  • Do not leave peripheral software running. KR ran 65 rounds of sanctions on 72,000 accounts, and legitimate players were caught with Logitech or Razer software in the background.

Playtest plan (September 17-18)

Section titled “Playtest plan (September 17-18)”

Pick 2-3 classes, not 8. Try both control modes (Aion 1 tab-target and the Aion 2 hybrid; the hybrid is recommended). Run Kraal Cave and Uruguru Canyon (and Fire Temple if time allows) as 5-player Expeditions. Follow the main story and use side quests only to top up XP. Do not chase the level cap or crafting: everything is wiped. The transmog Closet is not in the test.

Questions it answers: how the Templar macro chain feels, whether holding aggro is fun, whether watching party frames as a Cleric is tolerable, and your real ping in combat. Question it does not answer: which of the two is better in PvP.
