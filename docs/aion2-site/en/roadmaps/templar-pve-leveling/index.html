Templar: what to level at each level (PvE and leveling)

Region: global · Version: global client in Spanish from early access (2026-09-30 and 2026-10-01), with a Templar leveled from 1 to 23 · Reviewed: 2026-10-01 · Confidence: medium. The unlock levels, texts and numbers come from the client; the priority order is our recommendation, reasoned below, and draws on the leveling guides from the global test. What happens past level 23 we haven’t seen in-game yet.

This page is meant to stay open while you level. Every time you level up: open Skills, spend your points in the order below until the game won’t let you spend more, and check your level’s row in the table. The rotation (what to press and in what order) is on the sister page, Templar: rotation and macros (PvE).

The four rules that explain everything else

Section titled “The four rules that explain everything else”
  1. The game caps each skill by your level, not just by your points. On the skill card you’ll see “Adquirible en el nv. X” (“acquirable at level X”) when a skill can’t rank up any further at your current level. What we measured: Fierce Strike (Estacazo impetuoso) needed level 7 for its rank 4, and Blessing of Condemnation (Favor de condena) needed level 15 for its rank 4 — that’s roughly 6 levels after unlocking; we don’t have measurements for the higher ranks, but the pace is one rank every 2-3 levels. In practice, early on you’ll have points left over (at level 14 we were sitting on 18 unspent): the order below says where the leftover goes once your main skills are already maxed. This is a deduction from a handful of measurements, not a published rule.
  2. Skill points reset for free. Getting it wrong costs nothing: spread them out, try it, and if you don’t like it, reset. Daevanion nodes are bought with crystals, but refunding one costs 5,000 Kinah on the global client; the cost to reset stigmas isn’t known.
  3. Cost per rank: ranks 2 to 4 cost 1 point per rank, ranks 5 to 7 cost 2, and ranks 8 to 10 cost 4. Taking a skill from 1 to 8 is 13 points; from 1 to 10, 21. Passives cost the same. Points alone get you to 10; above that, you only rank up with the “+1” nodes on the Daevanion board, Arcana cards, and soulbind. That’s why the rank-12 and rank-16 specializations are endgame business, not leveling business.
  4. Where the points come from: every sealed dungeon (the yellow question mark on the map) gives +2 skill points and +2 Daevanion, and every green regional side quest gives +1 and +1. Don’t skip the green ones.

Spending order, as a list

Section titled “Spending order, as a list”

When you level up, go through this list top to bottom and rank up each one until the game won’t let you go further:

  1. Fierce Strike (Estacazo impetuoso) — left click. Your basic area attack and your mana source: every hit in its chain gives mana back. Since you press it constantly, every rank shows up in everything you do.
  2. Continuous Flurry (Azotamiento continuo) — right click. Your other area filler attack. It spends the mana the left click gives you, and its rank-12 specialization shortens Punishment’s (Castigo) cooldown.
  3. Punishment, from level 14. Your biggest hit, in an area and at range, and it gives you Executor (Ejecutante): +20% damage for 20s every 30s. With its 30s cooldown, if you fire it every time it’s back you carry the buff two-thirds of the time.
  4. Judgment (Juicio). It can only be used in a short window opened by your shield skills, but inside that window it’s your strongest hit against a single target: on the same level-23 character, around 2,000 damage, versus 850-1,170 per target for each hit in Fierce Strike’s chain.
  5. Shield Smite (Golpe de escudo). Stuns everything it hits (always on mobs), opens Judgment, and leaves mobs ready for Annihilation (Aniquilación). It’s the button that kicks off the whole combo.
  6. Annihilation, from level 12. A huge hit against a stunned or knocked-down enemy: on that same character, around 2,800. Cooldown 20s. Shield Smite and Shield of Ruin (Escudo de perdición) set it up.
  7. Protective Strike (Estacazo de amparo), from level 8. Area damage, heals you, gives you a damage-tolerance shield, and opens Judgment. It’s your in-combat healing.
  8. Passives, in this order as they unlock: Blessing of Condemnation (Favor de condena) (9) → Shock Hit (Acierto de impacto) (15) → Roar of Insult (Rugido agravante) (17) → Fury (Arrebato) (21, becomes the first one) → HP Boost (Mejora física). All other passives and actives go to the back of the queue: only once everything above is maxed for your level.

If you die a lot or spend the whole day drinking potions, level HP Boost before the damage passives: more HP and more healing received, and at rank 7 it already gives +1,750 flat HP and +22.5% max HP.

Specializations: which to pick

Section titled “Specializations: which to pick”

At rank 8 each skill offers you three options and you pick one; at rank 12 and 16 there’s a single, fixed option, but those ranks are only reached with the board’s extras, Arcanas, and soulbind: while leveling, the one you’re picking is the rank-8 one. Picking and slotting are two separate steps: after you pick it, drag it into the star slot. It’s free to change with a points reset.

Skill For soloing while leveling Against bosses / in groups Why

The ones that aren’t your main skills won’t reach rank 8 while leveling; only pick for the ones that do. The Korean guides say “get all actives to 8 first,” but on the global client the level cap doesn’t allow that until late, so here the seven main skills come first.

Stigmas: which ones, when, and how far

Section titled “Stigmas: which ones, when, and how far”

Four slots open up: 22, 27, 32 and 37. They’re leveled with Stigma Shards (roughly one per level from 22 on, plus ones from other sources), not with points, and resetting them may cost something: pick and don’t switch.

  • How much to level them: level 5 on each one first. Levels 1 to 5 cost 1 shard per level; 6 to 10 cost 2. Extra effects kick in at 5, 10, 15 and 20, and damage and duration go up every level (Shield of Ruin goes from ~2,050 to ~4,150 damage). Get all four to 5 before taking any further.
  • Don’t spread shards across all four at once. Finish one, then move to the next.
Slot Stigma (ES client) What it does Why this one
22 Shield of Ruin (Escudo de perdición) Charges up to 20m, knocks the target down (always on mobs), puts a barrier on you and opens Judgment for 3s, the longest window. Cooldown 30s Gets you into the group, sets up Annihilation (knockdown) and Judgment in one button. At level 5, landing it resets a cooldown — Annihilation’s, according to the Korean database (the client’s own text is cut off)
27 Sovereign’s Punishment (Castigo empíreo) 4m-radius area up to 20m away that stuns all 4 targets (always on mobs). Cooldown 1 min Opening a big group by stunning it all leaves everyone ready for Annihilation and buys you time
32 Executioner’s Sword (Espada de ejecución) Frontal area attack that lowers defense by 30% for 5s and hits 20% harder against stunned or knocked-down targets. Cooldown 45s Damage and defense shred, and your combo already leaves mobs stunned
37 Double Armor (Armadura doble) (solo) or Shield of Nezekan (Escudo de Nezekan) (group) Double Armor: +30% PvE damage tolerance for 10s. Shield of Nezekan: a barrier worth 41% HP for you and the group for 3s, meant for one specific big hit The fourth slot is the survival one. Solo, take your own; in a group, take the group one

Why these differ from Templar: what to level and when: that page also covers the group-tank reading; this one is for soloing by killing groups of mobs, which is why the 27 and 32 picks are damage and control. If you tank dungeons in a fixed group, the Korean tank reading swaps the 27 pick for Protective Shield (Escudo de protección) (guaranteed block) or Taunt (Provocación). Watch out: on the global client Taunt doesn’t have fixed threat, it only actually provokes players, so in PvE it’s worth taking for its damage and debuff.

Daevanion board

Section titled “Daevanion board”

Five boards open up: Nezekan at 12, Zikel at 20, Vaizel at 30, Triniel at 40, and Azphel at 45 (PvP only). Nodes are bought with Daevanion crystals, which come from the same sealed dungeons and green quests as skill points. Each node’s cost goes up as you progress (from 2 to 15 crystals), and refunding one costs 5,000 Kinah on the global client.

Priority order within each board:

  1. “+1 skill level” nodes for your main skills: Fierce Strike, Continuous Flurry, Judgment, Punishment, Shield Smite, Annihilation, Protective Strike, and passives like Blessing of Condemnation or Fury. A node like this is one more rank without spending points, it counts toward reaching the specializations (we saw this on Fierce Strike: the “(+1)” showed and the rank-8 one unlocked), and it’s one of the ways to go past 10. Whether it also skips the level cap hasn’t been checked.
  2. Extra attack and damage amplification that are on the way.
  3. Cooldown reduction and combat speed (Nezekan’s core): more Shield Smites, more Judgment windows.
  4. HP, defense and mana only as a stepping stone to a good node.
  5. Crit damage (Vaizel), last. A global-test guide says the Templar’s crit isn’t worth building around; it’s a single source, and Blessing of Condemnation (+crit) plus Judgment’s guaranteed crit at rank 12 might change that in the endgame.

Spend the crystals the same day you earn them: the board is a big chunk of your early power.

Where each “+1” node is

Section titled “Where each “+1” node is”

Every active skill has one “+1” node on each of the four boards (3 crystals each), so the boards take it to rank 14 at most (10 from points + 4); beyond that you need Arcana cards or soulbind. Each passive has four (2 crystals each), two of them on the same board. The table gives the position as the game shows it: r = row counted from the top and c = column from the left (Nezekan, Zikel and Vaizel are 11×11; Triniel is 13×13). Buying a whole board costs 134 crystals for Nezekan, 134 for Zikel, 134 for Vaizel and 168 for Triniel.

What not to do

Section titled “What not to do”
  • Leaving points unspent. If your main skills are maxed, the leftover goes to the next one on the list; it’s free to change later.
  • Leveling Capture, Shield Charge, Shock Release, or the defensive passives while you can still level a damage skill. They’re utility: they already do their job at rank 1, and they only get the leftovers.
  • Dodging normal hits. Blocking them triggers Fury (group damage), Shield of Protection (healing), and Weakening Strike (defense break).
  • Spreading Stigma Shards across several at once, or leveling one past 5 before all four are at 5.
