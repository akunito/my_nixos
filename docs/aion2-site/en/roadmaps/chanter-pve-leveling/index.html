Chanter: what to level at each level (PvE and leveling)

Region: global · Version: global client in Spanish from early access (2026-10-01), with a Chanter leveled to 14 · Reviewed: 2026-10-01 · Confidence: medium. The names, texts and unlocks you can read come from the client; the priority order is our recommendation for leveling, backed by the class’s Korean and Taiwanese guides and by the global test. Past level 14 we haven’t played the Chanter yet.

This page is meant to stay open while you level. Every time you level up: open Skills, spend your points in the order below until the game won’t let you spend more, and check your level’s row in the table. What to press and in what order is on the sister page, Chanter: rotation and macros (PvE).

What the Chanter is, in three sentences

Section titled “What the Chanter is, in three sentences”
  1. A mace melee that hits in an area, heals itself and heals the group. A timed leveling run in the global test took a Chanter from 1 to the cap in 4h 16m, faster than a Ranger, thanks to the constant area damage and self-healing.
  2. Its strong hit, Dark Crush (Fractura sombría), only comes out during a short window opened by its control skills, same as the Templar’s Judgment.
  3. In a group its value is in the mantras and buffs (Undefeated Mantra, Power of the Storm): they raise everyone’s damage, not just yours.

The rules that explain everything else

Section titled “The rules that explain everything else”
  1. The game caps each skill by your level. On the skill card you’ll see “Adquirible en el nv. X” (“acquirable at level X”) when it can’t rank up any further. Early on you’ll have points left over: the order below says where the leftover goes. (With the Templar we measured that rank 4 arrives about 6 levels after the skill unlocks; from that we infer one rank every 2-3 levels, but it’s a deduction. We haven’t measured it on the Chanter.)
  2. Skill points reset for free. Try things without worrying.
  3. Cost per rank: 1 point per rank from 2 to 4, 2 from 5 to 7, and 4 from 8 to 10. Going from 1 to 8 is 13 points; from 1 to 10, 21. Above 10 you only rank up with the “+1” nodes on the Daevanion board, Arcana cards, and soulbind; that’s why the rank-12 and rank-16 specializations are endgame business.
  4. Where the points come from: every sealed dungeon (the yellow question mark) gives +2 skill points and +2 Daevanion, and every green regional side quest gives +1 and +1.
  5. The Chanter’s problem while leveling is mana, not health. That’s why several of the recommendations below pick mana over damage until your gear improves.

Spending order, as a list

Section titled “Spending order, as a list”

When you level up, go through this list top to bottom and rank up each one until the game won’t let you go further:

  1. Incredible Blow (Fractura trituradora) — left click. Your basic area hit on 4 targets and your mana engine: its chain (Resonance Strike (Fractura resonante) → Thunderbolt Strike (Fractura fulminante)) gives you back 100 + 100 + 120 mana. One rank levels up all three hits at once.
  2. Dark Crush. Your strong hit against a single target, at 20m, but only within a window. Its damage scales a lot with rank (294 at rank 1; about 1,730 in the preview of the highest rank seen).
  3. Impact Strike (Fractura impactante). Stuns at 20m (always on mobs) and opens Dark Crush’s window for 2s. Ranking it up doesn’t lengthen the window: what it buys is its damage, and at rank 8 (13 points), the specialization that turns it into an area attack, stunning 4 mobs at once. That’s why it’s third, ahead of where the Korean guide puts it.
  4. Spin Strike (Golpe giratorio), from level 14. Area hit on 4 targets around one at 20m, the biggest hit in your kit at level 14 (about 2,600), gives you +15% crit damage for 2s and also opens Dark Crush’s window for 2s.
  5. Recuperation (Hechizo de recobro). Your heal: heals you and the group within 40m, removes a negative effect, and keeps healing for 8s. The Korean guides put it second; for soloing it’s enough that it doesn’t fall behind.
  6. Charge Strike (Trituración de acometida). A 20m charge that hits 4 and gives you back 100 mana. It’s how you get into each group.
  7. Incandescent Blow (Golpe incandescente) — right click — and Wave Blow (Golpe ondulatorio), from level 12. The right click costs 120 mana without giving any back: it’s filler, not a priority. Wave Blow knocks down a stunned target.
  8. Passives, as they unlock: Attack Readiness (Preparación de ataque) (13) → Blessing of Life (Bendición de vida) → Impact Hit (Acierto de impacto) (15) → Spell of Inspiration (Hechizo de inspiración) (11) → Spell of Fury (Hechizo de furia) (17) → Earth’s Promise (Promesa terrestre) (21).
  9. At rank 1 and only once nothing else needs points: Cross Guard (Defensa en cruz) (five independent Korean and Taiwanese guides say it isn’t worth the points) and Protective Ward (Círculo de protección) (the second one to skip; at rank 1 it already gives you a free barrier when you drop below 50% HP).

Specializations: which to pick

Section titled “Specializations: which to pick”

At rank 8 you pick one of three; at 12 and 16 a fixed one kicks in, but those ranks are endgame business. Picking and slotting are two separate steps: drag it into the star slot. Changing it is free with a reset.

Stigmas: which ones, when, and how far

Section titled “Stigmas: which ones, when, and how far”

Four slots: 22, 27, 32 and 37. They’re leveled with Stigma Shards (1 per level from 1 to 5, 2 from 6 to 10, 4 from 11 to 15, 8 from 16 to 20). Resetting them may cost something: pick and don’t switch.

The Chanter exception: on the other classes it’s best to take all four to level 5 before leveling any further. On the Chanter, every Korean guide says the opposite for Undefeated Mantra: max it out first, because it’s a buff for the whole group that grows with every level. Take the others to 5 and put the rest of your shards into Undefeated Mantra.

Slot Stigma (ES client) What it does Why this one
22 Undefeated Mantra (Proclama del invicto) Cost-free toggle: +10.5% PvE damage and +10.5% PvE damage tolerance for you and the nearby group, always on The one call every guide agrees on. It buffs your own damage too, so it’s just as good while soloing
27 Power of the Storm (Poder de vendaval) 10s of +20% combat speed and -20% cooldowns for you (and +20% / -10% for the group within 40m). Cooldown 2 min; at level 5, -30s More hits and more windows during those 10s. In a group, cast it before the fight so everyone opens with it
32 Marcutan’s Wrath (Ira de Marchutan) (solo) / Blocking Power (Poder de bloqueo) (group) Wrath: a 20m area attack that opens Dark Crush’s window for 3s. Blocking Power: a 16%-HP barrier for 20s for the whole group Solo, Marcutan’s Wrath is a third key to the window (and the longest one). In a group, the barrier saves wipes
37 Sprint Mantra (Proclama de esprint) (solo) / Barrier Spell (Hechizo de barrera) or Hand of Grace (Toque de recobro) (group) Sprint: +10.5% speed for you and the group plus healing on hit. Barrier: 3s during which nobody in the group drops below 10% HP Solo, moving faster between groups is time saved; in a group, the one that saves everyone

Why these differ from Chanter: what to level and when: that page has a “Solo and farming” column from a Korean guide from 11/2025: Undefeated Mantra, Sprint Mantra, Crushing Strike (Golpe devastador) and Extinguishing Flame (Extinción ígnea) or Assault Shock (Impacto de agresión). Here we change two things based on what the global client shows: Marcutan’s Wrath opens Dark Crush’s window for 3s (in Korea it only “armed” it), which turns it into pure damage for you, and Power of the Storm gives +20% combat speed and -20% cooldowns to yourself, not just the group. If you’d rather follow the Korean guide, Crushing Strike (20m charge, -30% target defense and +20% damage against stunned targets, 45s) is the damage alternative for slot 32 or 37. Extinguishing Flame (heavy area damage and knockdown) needs level 22 and completing the quest «Quien escucha la voz».

Daevanion board

Section titled “Daevanion board”

Five boards: Nezekan at 12, Zikel at 20, Vaizel at 30, Triniel at 40 and Azphel at 45 (PvP only). Nodes are bought with Daevanion crystals (from 2 to 15 each as you progress); refunding one costs 5,000 Kinah on the global client.

  1. “+1 skill level” nodes: one rank without spending points, and it counts toward the specializations. On the Global client there is one for almost the whole kit (17 seen: Incredible Blow, Incandescent Blow, Charge Strike, Impact Strike, Dark Crush, Windrage Fury, Scorch Strike, Recuperation, Vibration Strike, Wave Blow, Spin Strike, Shock Release and the passives Blessing of Life, Cross Guard, Protective Ward, Spell of Inspiration and Attack Readiness). Take your main skills’ first: Incredible Blow, Dark Crush, Impact Strike, Spin Strike, Recuperation and Attack Readiness; leave Cross Guard and Protective Ward for last.
  2. Additional Attack (+3), Critical (+5) and Casting Speed (+1.5%) that are on the way. The Taiwanese guides say not to prioritize cooldown reduction (+1.5%) on the Chanter.
  3. HP (+100), mana (+50), defense (+30) and critical resistance (+5) only as a step toward a good node.
  4. Don’t route your path through a node you don’t want, especially the Cross Guard one: going around it can cost about 15 points.
  5. Work the boards in parallel, don’t finish one before starting another.

Nezekan (the first, at 12) has combat speed and cooldown reduction as its core, and completing all of it costs 134 crystals. Nodes cost 2 to 14 crystals each, more the further from the start.

Where each “+1” node is

Section titled “Where each “+1” node is”

Every active skill has one “+1” node on each of the four boards (3 crystals each), so the boards take it to rank 14 at most (10 from points + 4); beyond that you need Arcana cards or soulbind. Each passive has four (2 crystals each), two of them on the same board. The table gives the position as the game shows it: r = row counted from the top and c = column from the left (Nezekan, Zikel and Vaizel are 11×11; Triniel is 13×13). Buying a whole board costs 134 crystals for Nezekan, 134 for Zikel, 134 for Vaizel and 168 for Triniel.

What not to do

Section titled “What not to do”
  • Spending mana on the right click when you’re tight on it. The left click gives it back; the right one doesn’t.
  • Putting points into Cross Guard or Protective Ward.
  • Spreading shards across stigmas, or leaving Undefeated Mantra stuck at 5 once the others are already at 5.
  • Expecting to feel like a DPS in your group. In a group, your job is buffing, and that doesn’t show up in your numbers.
