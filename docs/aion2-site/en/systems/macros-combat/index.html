Combat, macros, targeting and controls

Region: Korea and Taiwan, plus the Global Launch Scale Test of 2026-09-17/19 · Version: up to 2026-09-22 · Reviewed: 2026-09-22 · Confidence: medium. The macro’s rules are now confirmed on a Global client; every damage measurement is still Korean or Taiwanese.

Summary

Section titled “Summary”
  • The macro is not optional min-maxing, it is how Aion 2 is played. Every class guide in the corpus, for all eight classes, builds a macro first and a rotation second. You hold 1-2 mouse buttons and 4-6 situational skills stay manual.
  • The rules below were confirmed on the Global client in the September playtest; the numbers were not. The macro, its top-down priority, the 4 Stigma slots of Season 1, which cap how much of your burst can be automated, and the settings all come from Launch Scale Test footage. Every damage figure is still Korean or Taiwanese, and Season 1 ships an earlier build with its own balance pass.
  • There are TWO macro layers. NC’s official in-game skill macro, added to KR/TW around 2026-01-28 under Settings → Key Settings → General/Gameplay → Macro, and the external hardware macros (G HUB, Razer Synapse) that top players run on top of it.
  • Since the 2026-08-26 Global Cooldown patch you probably do not need a gaming mouse. A Cleric ran two 1-minute parses: hardware macro ~5.0M damage and 537 hits, in-game macro only “almost the same”. It is the only direct A/B that exists.
  • Animation cancelling (“평캔”, weaving) is worth 10-12% on a full rotation, not 2x. Templar on TW: ~5.5M with cancelling against 4.9M without. Tuning a Ranger’s cancel bar on KR gave +6%.
  • The confirmation cue is a black shadow on your character. Six independent creators across KR and TW use it as the signal that the delay is tuned correctly.
  • Templar and Cleric are both macro-friendly and neither is ping-sensitive. Templar’s damage is a Judgment cancel chain; Cleric’s is a held 3-4 skill macro with heals left outside. Assassin and Sorcerer are the ping-punished picks, not these two.
  • Copy the structure, never the milliseconds. Delay scales with ping: 10 ms under 50, 40-50 ms at 80-100 and above. The in-game default is 30 ms. Copying a Taiwanese creator’s 10 ms at 200 ping is copying a workaround, not a setting.
  • Three settings do more for your comfort than any tuning. Turn OFF “track target when using skill” for solo play (melee skills walk you up to 4 m), and leave it ON only when you hold a macro against moving targets; move Defiance and the break-CC key off the spacebar; bind “target hostile players” to the middle mouse button for the Abyss.
  • Never put in a gap-closer, a charge skill or a heal. A dash inside a held macro walks you into the boss one-shot; a macro’d 3-stage charge fires at stage 1 and loses most of its damage.
  • Expect to rebuild the macro repeatedly. Gladiator’s cancel mechanic flipped three times in 10 days (2026-08-26 to 09-05) and Chanter’s core rotation four times in two months, twice in one week.

How it works

Section titled “How it works”

Combat is a tab-target hybrid wearing action-combat clothes. You have a 12-slot skill bar, but several skills share one key and the macro layer decides which of them fires. In tab-target mode your character keeps auto-attacking between macro steps, which sustains mana for free; in action-combat mode each skill halts you, so you have to insert an auto-attack line into the macro yourself. Three ideas stack on top of that. Chains: left-click is a 2-3 hit basic chain that generates mana, right-click a heavier 2-hit chain that spends it, and each link carries an activation percentage you can read by hovering the skill in the K menu. Animation cancelling: every skill has a recovery tail and starting the next input during it truncates the tail, so the same rotation fits more hits in; moving Arrow Barrage’s position in a Ranger’s cancel bar raised its hit count from 12 to 30. And priority resolution: the macro reads down your chain and picks the first legal, off-cooldown skill, which is why proc-gated abilities sit above the filler.

A macro line is therefore a priority combo chain, not a rotation. It resolves in two steps: first the combo order you defined, then the skill’s position among those whose cooldown is up. Any manual keypress instantly overrides the loop and it resumes when you let go. The key must be held, not tapped. Only active skills and Stigmas can go in, never passives, and the macro mirrors whichever of the 3 skill-bar presets is active, so switching preset switches what it fires.

The delay is the hinge, and almost everyone misreads it as a speed knob: it is a synchronisation knob. Each step has to arrive at the server while the previous skill is still in its cancellable window. Too short and inputs get eaten; too long and you lose the cancel. That is why the correct value tracks your round-trip time, why the shadow is the tuning signal, and why a staggered 50/53/52/51 ms pattern out-performs a flat 50 ms. PC performance enters the same equation: the same macro under-performs on a weak machine because the client cannot present the frames the window is measured in. The whole system exists because of a failure: at KR/TW launch anyone with a programmable mouse out-damaged everybody else, and gold-farming workshops ran AFK loops from the client’s own target lock plus one key. NC answered with enforcement (CAPTCHA, VPN blocking, hardware-based restrictions, roughly 50,000 permanent bans, real-name verification) and with shipping an official macro on 2026-01-28 while banning the third-party tools it replaced. It did not fully work: every top guide in 2026 still shows a G HUB or Razer layer stacked on top, described by a Korean specialist as having drifted “into the allowed range”, and no source addresses whether that is ToS-compliant.

Contested or changed by patch

Skill Reservation (스킬 예약, “skill queueing” on the Global client) splits per skill, not per class: a Sorcerer demonstration shows that with Ice Chain in the kit it has to be OFF or the other skills silently fail to fire, and that without Ice Chain it is better ON, because manually pressed crowd control then goes off while the macro keeps running. On the Global build two settings guides recommend OFF for everybody, since it adds input latency and fights animation cancelling. Fire order is settled: the Global client fires the column top-down, 0 → 1 → 2 → 3, skipping any skill whose condition is not met, so proc-gated abilities go above the filler. The entry cap is 20 per three sources measured against an actual error message, though one July source guesses “about 50, I think”. The anti-cancel position is held only by a 2025-11 Cleric guide that predates the macro system: treat it as obsolete. On 2026-08-26 KR added a Global Cooldown and with it in-game-macro-only play reached parity with hardware, and that is the single most important unknown for Global, because Season 1 is described as roughly KR before Chapter 1, that is, earlier than that date. One Taiwanese creator claims a 2026-09 network rebalance giving higher-ping players more damage (20-30% for Gladiator), but it is a single source in a video sponsored by a VPN. And on 2026-09-16 Korea went after the technique itself: the patch note increases the cancel delay on Templar’s Continuous Flurry (also translated Continuous Onslaught), Punishing Strike and Judgment, saying in as many words that it targets players macro-cancelling into Judgment, and does the same to three Assassin skills. The Assassin “main cancel” died with it, and the Korean board reads the whole 08-26 to 09-16 chain as a net Templar damage loss. None of this is in the Global build yet.

Builds

Section titled “Builds”

Templar, the highest-value macro in the game

Section titled “Templar, the highest-value macro in the game”

Templar’s damage is almost entirely a Judgment-uptime problem, and Judgment is proc-fed by Warding Strike, Shield Smite and Doom Shield, which is exactly what a priority chain is good at.

  • PvX and open world (TW, 2026-09): right-click chain Doom Shield → Annihilate (knockdown-gated) → Debilitating Smash (proc-gated) → Pummel (filler), plus a separate side-button macro running Warding Strike → Judgment.
  • PvE dungeon (TW, 2026-09): right-click chain Annihilate → Debilitating Smash → Flash Rampage → Pummel, and a macro on R running Judgment → Shield Smite (+2 s Judgment) → Warding Strike (+2 s) → Doom Shield (+3 s), looping as Shield Smite returns. Open with Punishment, then hold.
  • Berserk variant (TW, 2026-09): hardware turbo on R running Judgment > Shield Smite > Warding Strike > Doom Shield, dropping the Taunt and Block Stigmas because the macro’s damage holds aggro on its own.
  • Two Korean schools (2026-09-02): 우심 or center-lock (positional, Punishment always full-charge, drains mana) against 좌심 or left-center (flow-based, no drain, you can heal). Both land around 6.1M and the conclusion is that PC and region variance exceeds the difference between the schools.
  • Always manual: Punishment (3-stage charge), Executing Blade in duels, Block, Parry, the party shield and gap-closers.

Cleric, macro the damage and hand-play the healing

Section titled “Cleric, macro the damage and hand-play the healing”

Unanimous across five sources: heals stay manual and reactive, and only the damage loop is automated.

  • The damage engine, stated mechanically: Earth’s Punishment leaves a 10-second mark; while it is up, Condemnation critically hits every time and each crit resets its own cooldown, so the loop is mark, then spam Condemnation. A level-5 Stigma is what guarantees the crit.

  • Global-facing PvE (2026-09): left-click Earth Retribution and macro Earth Punishment → Noble Aura → Divine Aura. The min-max version stacks the four buffs on one key, then macros Noble Aura → buffs on cooldown → Chain of Torment → Condemnation → Judgment Thunder, keeping Debilitating Mark and Divine Aura manual.

  • Tested minimum-viable macro (KR, 2026-06): 2x Judgment Thunder + 1x Condemnation, around 260k DPS with no mana regeneration in the loop. Explicit advice: retest it after every ~100k combat power gained.

  • Chain-percentage method (TW, 2026-03): keep only links near 100%. Dropping a 15% third hit took his Cleric from 1,348 to 2,826 DPS.

  • Hybrid healer macro (TW, 2026-07): keep toggles (Light of Protection) and easy-to-forget long-cooldown skills (Benevolence, 1:30) in, and Bolt, a charge skill, out. Macro spam drains mana noticeably faster than manual play.

  • Cross-class trick worth stealing: hold the heal key through the damage macro press instead of tapping it; the heal then fires in the gaps between auto-attacks without needing Skill Reservation on.

For the other six classes the shape varies a lot: the Ranger performs best with a single-skill macro, the Sorcerer runs a 9-line one with Hellfire always hand-charged, and the Assassin stays at 2 skill keys and 2 Stigmas.

Mistakes and traps

Section titled “Mistakes and traps”
  • Putting a gap-closer in a held macro: it dashes you into a boss’s telegraphed one-shot while you are mashing.
  • Macroing a charge skill: it fires at stage 1. The only workaround is a press-and-hold bind with no key-up event.
  • Copying a creator’s millisecond value: it encodes their ping (often 170-200 ms on TW) and their PC.
  • Stuffing everything into one macro: a cycling loop wastes presses on cooled-down or already-toggled skills, and a second row measurably reduces how often each skill in the macro fires. One row, and split the overflow onto the mouse macro.
  • Including a low chain-percentage link: a 15% link more than halved a Cleric’s dummy DPS, and runes lifting it to 30% do not fix it.
  • Leaving Skill Reservation on by default: for several classes it silently prevents the skill from firing at all.
  • Leaving “track target when using skill” on for solo content with dodge timings: melee skills auto-walk you up to 4 m for no combo benefit.
  • Re-casting a maintenance debuff “just in case”: the animation costs more than the uptime gained.
  • Treating the macro as a rotation: it is a sequencer. You open with buffs and burst by hand, then hold.
  • Forgetting mana: several builds fold in a rune or a mana-generating filler specifically so the button can be held.

Open questions

Section titled “Open questions”

Does Global Season 1 include the 2026-08-26 Global Cooldown? That decides whether an in-game-only macro is competitive, which is to say whether you need a programmable mouse at all. The 2026-09-17/19 playtest left it unanswered: no Launch Scale Test source mentions a global cooldown at all, and a Taiwanese review from 09-20 states flatly that its own client has none. So the GCD is a Korean fact, its absence a Taiwanese one, and Global is unknown. Check it in Advanced Access, the early access from 09-30. The playtest settled two other questions: the macro fires top-down, and Season 1 equips 4 Stigmas (the first at 22, then 27, 32 and 37). Still open: the entry cap on Global, where 20 is best attested. Is an external hardware macro against Global’s ToS? No source says, and the only official sentence about enforcement (2026-01-28) names VPN blocking, reporting and hardware restrictions, nothing about input automation: read Global’s ToS before building one. Is the Taiwanese network rebalance real? Does animation cancelling work on casters? Korean sources say the left-click cancels and the right-click does not, which implies it is per-skill rather than per-class. And there is nothing on Skill Reservation for Templar and Cleric specifically, the two classes this site covers in depth.
